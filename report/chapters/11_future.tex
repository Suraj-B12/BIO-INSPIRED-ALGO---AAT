\chapter{Future Work}
\section{Test on field photos}
The most important next step is to test the model on tomato leaf photos taken in real fields. PlantDoc and Field-PV show why this matters~\cite{singh2020plantdoc,gui2021field}. Photos from different phones, places, disease stages, and leaves with more than one problem should be included. The model should also learn to say ``I don't know'' for leaves or plants it was not trained on.

\section{Make the search faster}
Multi-fidelity methods give more training time to promising recipes and stop weak ones early. DEHB is one example~\cite{awad2021dehb}. SHADE and L-SHADE adapt DE's own settings and population size while searching~\cite{tanabe2013shade,tanabe2014lshade}. Any such change should be compared with the same fair rules used here.

\section{Multi-objective, robustness-aware search}
Optimising clean accuracy alone does not guarantee robustness or good calibration. A natural extension is a multi-objective DE whose fitness also includes accuracy on corrupted validation images, calibration error, speed, and model size. Such a search returns a set of best trade-offs (a Pareto front) to choose from. Simple fixes such as temperature scaling for calibration and noise augmentation during training can also be added. For phone use, the model can be exported and timed on the target device; compact networks such as MobileNet offer further options~\cite{sandler2018mobilenet,howard2019mobilenet}.

\section{Stronger evidence and user feedback}
Repeating the whole CNN search with different seeds would show how much the search itself varies. Plant experts could review the Grad-CAM maps. Feedback from farmers and extension workers could guide the collection of new, difficult images for future training and testing.
