\chapter{Comparative Performance Analysis}
\section{DE versus random search}
DE and random search used exactly the same settings space, data, fitness measure, number of trials, and final selection step. The only difference is how each one proposes new recipes, so this is a fair test of the search strategy itself.

\ArtifactFigureShort{fig_de_vs_rs}{Equal-budget DE versus random search}{Best validation macro-F1 found so far against the number of trials, for DE and random search.}{fig:ders}

During the search, DE found higher-scoring recipes than random search (\Cref{fig:ders}), showing that its population steers trials toward good regions. After full-size retraining, random search's chosen candidate scored slightly higher on validation, and on the final test both strategies reach \DEAcc\% accuracy and \DEFone\% macro-F1. So DE matched a strong random-search control on the final test while leading during the search itself. With only seven settings, \NumEvals\ random trials already cover the space well; DE's guided search is likely to matter more in larger search spaces.

Against the baseline, the comparison covers the whole recipe, including the switch from Adam to AdamW. Published PlantVillage studies~\cite{mohanty2016plant,ferentinos2018plant,too2019plant} and evolutionary-search studies~\cite{real2019evolution,awad2021dehb} use different classes, splits, and computing budgets, so they serve as context rather than a direct ranking.
